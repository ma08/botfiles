What happened
Levent Alpöge, a number theorist at Anthropic, posted a 4-page note saying he and Claude found a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, and of a broader version (GRH for Dirichlet L-functions). The proof is 17.4 million lines of Lean, a language computers can check, with zero comments. Nobody has a human-readable proof yet.

What RH says
The primes are about as regular as they could possibly be.
"Regular" here means: estimate how many primes there are below x with a smooth formula, li(x), and you're never off by much more than √x. (Precisely: at most about √x·log x, for every x. And the error can't be much smaller than √x, so that's the best case.)
Below a trillion there are about 37.6 billion primes, and the formula misses by only about 38,000.
Riemann guessed this in 1859. Computers have checked more than 10 trillion zeros. Nobody had proved it.

The idea, per the note
- In the finite-field version of RH, Stepanov's trick works: build a polynomial that vanishes with high multiplicity
- Multiplicity needs derivatives
- You can't differentiate whole numbers (the derivative of 7 is just 0)
- Workaround: chain "theta lifts", each one mimicking one more order of multiplicity
- About √X of them. Per the note, that number was Claude's suggestion

What's been checked (as of Nov 21, per Andrew Sutherland's verification page and the Lean community forum)
- All 175,187 files compile, in several independent builds
- The 5 main theorems use only Lean's 3 standard axioms
- Full kernel replay: zero rejections
- 6+ independent proof checkers accept it
- One last official comparison run is expected over the weekend
