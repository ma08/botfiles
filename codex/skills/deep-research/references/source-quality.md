# Source Quality Rules

## Trust tiers

### Tier 1 (highest trust)
- Official government/statutory datasets and filings
- Primary regulations/laws/standards
- Company filings and official disclosures
- Peer-reviewed research and established institutional publications

### Tier 2 (moderate trust)
- Reputable national/business media
- Established industry reports with transparent methodology
- Major research organizations with clear sourcing

### Tier 3 (low trust)
- Aggregator blogs without primary links
- User-generated forums or unverified social posts
- SEO-content farms and scraped mirrors

Use Tier 3 only as directional context. Do not base core conclusions on Tier 3 evidence.

## Citation rules
- Cite every load-bearing claim.
- Prefer direct links to original sources.
- Include publication/update date where relevant.
- If a source is indirect, mark it as indirect.

## Conflict resolution
When sources disagree:
1. Prefer higher trust tier.
2. Prefer the most recent source when recency matters.
3. Prefer sources with transparent methodology.
4. Keep dissenting evidence visible in limitations if unresolved.

## Confidence framework
Use: `High`, `Medium`, `Low`.

- High: corroborated by multiple Tier 1/2 sources with no major conflict.
- Medium: partially corroborated, minor conflicts, or limited primary data.
- Low: sparse evidence, reliance on proxies, or unresolved conflicts.

## Evidence vs inference
For each major recommendation:
- State evidence explicitly (what the sources say).
- State inference explicitly (your interpretation).
- Avoid mixing them in one sentence when stakes are high.
