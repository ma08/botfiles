# Regular Artifact Style

Use this mode for task context, issue or PR families, product plans, decision briefs, and general explainers.

The inspiration is the frameworkless readability of Andrej Karpathy's official blog: simple HTML/CSS, narrow prose, strong headings, ordinary links, and content rather than interface chrome. Do not copy branding or exact layout. Reference: `https://karpathy.ai/blog/index.html`.

## Visual grammar

- Use a white or lightly warm background, near-black text, ordinary blue links, and subtle gray metadata.
- Keep the article column around 680–760 px. Allow figures and tables to break out modestly when needed.
- Use a system sans-serif or readable serif stack; avoid remote fonts.
- Prefer paragraphs, headings, lists, code blocks, blockquotes, images, and horizontal rules.
- Use one small header with title and metadata. Avoid sticky navigation unless the artifact is genuinely long.
- Avoid card grids, pills, gradients, shadows, hero metrics, ornamental icons, and app-like controls.

## Recommended article structure

1. Title and one-sentence orientation.
2. Short answer or recommendation.
3. Context needed to understand the answer.
4. One or two diagrams or tables where prose is insufficient.
5. Decisions, tradeoffs, and next action.
6. Sources and validation notes.

## Diagrams

- Prefer compact inline SVG, a simple table, or a small sequence diagram.
- Use one accent color and label arrows directly.
- Place a plain-language caption below the visual.
- Do not visualize a list merely to make the page look designed.

## Readability checks

- A reader should understand the answer without interacting with the page.
- Keep paragraphs short but not fragmentary.
- Do not hide core evidence in accordions.
- On mobile, preserve type size and stack tables or permit localized table scrolling without page overflow.
